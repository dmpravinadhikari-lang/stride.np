**Refusals are not random.** Read fifty refusal letters and the same small set of causes keeps appearing. That is good news, because a pattern can be checked for before a file is lodged rather than explained afterwards.

## The three that account for most of it

**Money that cannot be explained.** Not insufficient money. Unexplained money. A balance that appeared shortly before the application, from a source nobody can document, reads as borrowed for the purpose. The fix is boring and effective: trace every significant deposit to something with paperwork behind it, and where a relative is funding, have the relationship and the source documented properly.

**A course that does not follow.** A student with a commerce background applying for a hospitality diploma is not disqualified, but they are asked to explain it, and "it was cheaper" is not an explanation that works. If the jump is real, the file must argue it deliberately.

**The file and the student disagreeing.** This is the one consultancies cause. A statement written in a vocabulary the student does not use, which they then cannot defend when asked a simple question about it. An officer does not need to prove anything. They only need to doubt.

## Check the file as though it were hostile

Build a check that happens before lodgement, done by somebody who did not write the file. That second pair of eyes is the whole mechanism.

What that person is looking for:

- **Names and dates that disagree** across citizenship, academic records, passport and application. The single most common avoidable defect.
- **Gaps in study or work** that are unexplained. Gaps are fine. Unexplained gaps are not.
- **Financial documents** whose story does not hold together.
- **A statement** that uses words the student would not use.
- **Course and destination** consistent with what the student actually said they wanted.

Whoever checks this should be able to stop a lodgement. If the check is advisory, it will be overruled at the end of the month.

## Rehearse the interview, do not script it

For destinations that interview, a rehearsal is the highest value hour in the process, and it is commonly skipped because there is no fee attached.

Rehearse the things that are actually asked: why this course, why this country, who is paying and how, what you intend to do afterwards. The aim is not a polished answer. It is that the student has said it out loud once before saying it to someone who can refuse them.

Scripted answers are worse than unprepared ones. Officers interview all day and recognise recitation immediately.

## Keep the refusals and read them

Most offices file a refusal away and move on, which throws away the only reliable information available about what is actually being rejected.

Keep them. Once a quarter read them together as a team. The pattern that emerges is usually specific and fixable, and it is frequently one counsellor or one destination rather than the office as a whole.

Two questions for each refusal: could we have seen this before lodging, and did anything in our own process contribute? The second question is uncomfortable, and the offices that ask it are the ones whose rate improves.

## The student you turn away

The strongest protection for your success rate is the file you decline to lodge.

This is commercially painful and it is the correct decision. A refusal follows that student onto every future application to any country. A consultancy that lodges it anyway has taken a fee and reduced a young person's options, and word of that travels in a way no advertising can repair.

Have the honest conversation instead. Sometimes the answer is a different destination, sometimes a year of work and saving, sometimes a different course. Students remember who told them the truth, and they have younger siblings.
