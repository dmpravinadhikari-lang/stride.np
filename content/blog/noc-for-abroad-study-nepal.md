**The NOC is the Ministry of Education's written permission for you to study abroad, and without it your bank cannot legally send your tuition out of Nepal.** As at August 2026 you apply entirely online at [noc.moest.gov.np](https://noc.moest.gov.np), pay NPR 2,000, and receive a digitally signed certificate with a QR code in one to five working days.

That is the whole answer. The rest of this page is the detail that decides whether it takes three days or three weeks.

## What the NOC actually is

The No Objection Certificate, everyone calls it the NOC, is issued by the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology. It says the Ministry has no objection to you leaving to study at a named institution, on a named course.

Two things depend on it, and both are hard stops:

- **Your money cannot leave the country without it.** Under Nepal Rastra Bank's foreign exchange rules, a bank will not remit tuition abroad for a student who has no NOC. Not "will be reluctant to", cannot.
- **Your visa file is incomplete without it.** Posts assessing Nepali applicants expect to see it.

This is why the NOC sits in the same week as your fee payment, not at the end of the process.

## What you need before you start

| Document | What trips people up |
|---|---|
| Offer letter from a recognised institution | It must be the institution's own letter, not an agent's summary of it |
| Academic transcripts and certificates | SEE, +2, and your bachelors if you have one |
| Citizenship certificate | Both sides, and the name must match your passport exactly |
| Valid passport | Check the expiry covers your course plus a margin |
| Equivalence certificate | Only if your qualification is from outside Nepal |

**The single most common cause of delay is a name mismatch.** "Sujata Gurung" on the citizenship, "Sujata Kumari Gurung" on the transcript and "GURUNG SUJATA" on the passport is three different people as far as a verifying officer is concerned. Fix that before you upload anything. It is far slower to fix afterwards.

## The process, step by step

1. **Register** on the portal in your own name, with an email you will still have in two years.
2. **Fill the application**, personal details, institution, course, country, intake.
3. **Upload the documents** as clear, complete scans. Photograph the whole page in daylight; a cropped or shadowed scan gets sent back.
4. **Pay NPR 2,000** through ConnectIPS, eSewa or Khalti.
5. **Submit**, then watch the portal for status changes.
6. **Download** the signed PDF. It carries a QR code that anyone can scan to verify it.

Keep the PDF. Your bank will want it, your consultancy will want it, and the post may want it.

## What it costs, honestly

**NPR 2,000 to the Ministry.** That is the government fee as at August 2026.

If someone quotes you NPR 8,000 or NPR 15,000 "for the NOC", you are being charged a service fee for someone doing the online form on your behalf. That is a legitimate thing to sell, and some students are glad to pay it. But you should know which part is the government's fee and which part is theirs, and you should be told that before you pay, not after.

## Where students actually lose time

- **Applying before the offer is firm.** If your course or start date changes, the NOC has to be redone.
- **Uploading a partial scan.** Half a citizenship certificate is the commonest resubmission.
- **A name that varies across documents.** Sort it out first.
- **Leaving it until the fee deadline.** The NOC gates the bank transfer. If your fee is due on the 20th and you start the NOC on the 18th, you have given yourself no room.
- **Losing access to the account.** If the registration used somebody else's email, retrieving it later is painful. Register in the student's own name.

## Where the NOC sits in the wider process

The NOC is not the first step and it is not the last. In practice:

**Offer letter → tuition deposit decision → NOC → bank remittance → visa lodgement.**

Because it gates the money, it should be started the moment your offer and your intake are firm. Not once everything else is done.
