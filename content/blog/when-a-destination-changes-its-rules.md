**Every consultancy in Nepal has had the month where a destination changed its rules and half the pipeline stopped being viable.** Funds requirements rise, a visa pathway narrows, a post study work right is trimmed. It arrives without notice, and it lands on students who have already paid you.

You cannot prevent it. You can be the office that handled it well, and that is worth more than the intake you lost.

## Find out first, from the source

The worst position is learning about a change from a student who read it on Facebook.

Subscribe directly to the immigration department feeds for every country you place into, and read them yourself. Partners and aggregators will send a summary, but it arrives after they have worked out their own position, and by then your families have been anxious for two days.

Watch the country's own news pages rather than commentary. Commentary is usually accurate about the direction and wrong about the detail, and the detail is what decides whether your particular students are affected.

## The first forty eight hours

**Work out who is actually affected.** Not "our Australia students", which is panic. Which students, at which stage, with which intake. A student whose visa is already granted is usually unaffected. A student about to lodge may be. A student still at counselling has options.

This requires being able to list your students by destination and stage within minutes. Offices that cannot do this lose the first two days to producing the list, which is exactly when families are deciding whether to trust them.

**Write one clear message, then personalise it.** Same facts to everyone, with a line specific to each student's position. Send it before they ask.

**Say what you do not yet know.** "The requirement is changing from this date. We do not yet know how it applies to applications already lodged, and we are asking. We will tell you on Sunday either way." That is a message people respect. Silence while you wait for certainty is what loses them.

## What to say to a family that has already paid

Directly, and with the options laid out: wait for the next intake under the new rules, switch destination, or stop and be refunded according to a policy you can show them.

Have that refund policy written before you need it. Deciding it during a crisis, student by student, produces inconsistent outcomes, and students compare notes.

The families who stay are almost never the ones who got the best outcome. They are the ones who felt informed.

## Stop one country deciding your year

An office where most students go to one destination is not a consultancy, it is a bet.

Spreading is not free. Each additional destination means a counsellor who genuinely knows its requirements, its documents and its timelines, and covering eight badly is worse than covering three properly.

A workable target: no single destination carrying more than about a third of your pipeline, and at least one of your destinations on a different policy cycle from the others. When you notice one country climbing past that share, that is the moment to build the next one, not when the rules change.

## The quiet advantage afterwards

Policy changes remove weak operators from the market. An office that placed students into one destination on thin files and thin advice does not survive the month.

If you handled it well, you come out of it with families who tell other families, and often with counsellors from offices that did not. The consultancies that grew fastest after the last few rounds of changes were rarely the biggest before them. They were the ones who answered the phone.
