**Ask an owner why a student did not enrol and you will usually hear that they went to a cheaper consultancy.** Look at the actual files and a different pattern appears. The student came in, was interested, was promised a call, and the call came a week later or not at all.

They did not choose a competitor. They were never given a reason to choose you.

## The shape of the problem

It is almost never the first conversation that fails. A walk in gets attention, because they are standing there. The failure is in the gap afterwards, and the gap has a predictable shape.

A student asks about Australia in Asar. They need their IELTS result, so there is nothing to do for six weeks. The counsellor makes a mental note. Three weeks later that counsellor is deep in an intake deadline for four other students. The result arrives and nobody knows, because nobody was watching for it.

In Mangsir the student walks past a different office and someone there asks them a question.

Nothing went wrong. Nobody was careless. The office simply had no mechanism for remembering something six weeks out, and human memory is not that mechanism once you pass about thirty live files.

## Three gaps worth measuring

You do not need analytics to find these. You need to look at last week.

**Enquiry to first contact.** How long between someone giving you their number and someone calling it. If this is more than a day, fix it before anything else. It is the cheapest improvement available and it changes the student's impression of you permanently.

**Promise to delivery.** A counsellor said "I will send you the fee breakdown tomorrow." Did it go tomorrow? This one is invisible in most offices, because the promise was made verbally and lives nowhere.

**Quiet file to next contact.** Of the students who have not been spoken to in three weeks, how many are still genuinely live? Most offices discover the number is higher than they expected, and that a third of them still want to go.

## What actually recovers a quiet file

Not a bulk message. Students recognise one and it confirms they are a name on a list.

What works is specific and small: a message that references their destination, their course, and the thing they were waiting for. "Your IELTS result should be through by now, shall I look at the two Melbourne options we discussed?" That is not clever. It just proves someone remembers, which almost nobody does.

The obstacle is not willingness. It is that recalling that detail for two hundred students is impossible unless it was written down at the time, next to the student, with a date attached.

## The fix, in the order it is worth doing

**Write the next action down, always, at the moment it is agreed.** Not "follow up" but what and when. "Call 12 Asar about IELTS result." A note without a date is not a task.

**Put it where the office can see it, not the counsellor.** The test is simple: if that counsellor were away for a week, could somebody else pick up their five most urgent students without ringing them? If not, your office does not own its own pipeline.

**Look at the overdue list every morning, together.** Five minutes. Whose promises are late. Not to blame anyone, because usually the answer is that the person is overloaded and the useful response is to move something, not to chase harder.

**Stop counting enquiries and start counting stages.** Twenty enquiries is not information. Twenty enquiries of which eleven have an offer, four are waiting on a visa, and five have not been contacted in a month tells you what to do today.

## Why this is worth more than more marketing

An office spending on advertising while failing to call back the people it already attracted is filling a bucket with a hole in it. The marketing works. The students arrive. They are simply not being caught.

Fixing follow up costs nothing except the discipline of writing things down somewhere shared. It also compounds, because a student who was called back when they were promised tells other students, and in Nepal that is still how most consultancies actually grow.

Before you increase the budget, spend one afternoon on the enquiries from three months ago. Count how many were never contacted a second time. That number is usually the most expensive thing in the office, and it does not appear on any report.
